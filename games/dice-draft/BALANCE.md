# Dice Draft — balance report

Generated by `node sim/balance.js --write`. 100,000 seeded matches per pairing (seeds 1–100000), every
one played through the authoritative match layer (`js/match.js`), Classic pay table unless stated.
Win / draw / loss figures are from the first-named player's side.

## Verdict

| Check | Result | Headline |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Solver beats random | PASS | wins 98.8%, mean gap +30.6 pts |
| 2. Solver beats greedy | PASS | wins 70.1%, loses 17.8%, mean gap +3.7 pts |
| 3. No dominant zone | PASS | largest share of solver points 20.2%, smallest 7.3%; top first-roll home 19.4% |
| 4. Mirror match is not a tie | NOTE | identical solvers draw 100% (by construction); a solver that reads the scoreboard beats the pure-odds solver 35.9% to 21.9% |
| 5. No seat advantage | PASS | swapping seats mirrors results exactly; random-vs-random seat A 49.0% vs seat B 49.1% |

Two things to read before relying on this:

- **Check 2 passes, but the ceiling above a simple heuristic is low.** Greedy play still scores 94% of
  the solver's points, and the one-step "Grinder" heuristic scores 99%. Tuning point values moved this only
  a little (see "What tuning changed"). The limit is structural: zones score independently, so there is
  not much for deep planning to find. See Recommendations.
- **Check 4 does not hold as the brief wrote it.** Two players with the same information and the same
  deterministic policy place every roll identically and draw every time. That is true of any
  shared-sequence game. What the data does show is that the match is not a pure solitaire race: a player
  who watches the opponent's board and plays for the win, not the average, beats the pure-odds solver.

## 3. Zone balance (solver play)

Every match fills all eight zones once, so "share of placements" is 12.5% each by construction. The
useful questions are where the points come from, how often a zone is sacrificed, and where the solver
sends a roll when everything is open.

| Zone | Rule | Pays | Share of points | Mean pts | Scores zero | Mean turn filled | Filled last | First-roll home |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | Total 4–10, no triple | 2 pts per pip under 11 | 12.3% | 7.07 | 6.1% | 4.11 | 11.0% | 16.7% |
| Big | Total 11–17, no triple | 2 pts per pip over 10 | 12.5% | 7.23 | 7.7% | 4.36 | 12.4% | 11.1% |
| Triple | All 3 dice match | 30 pts | 8.4% | 4.83 | 83.9% | 5.86 | 14.5% | 2.8% |
| Double | Any two dice match | 10 pts | 16.4% | 9.45 | 5.5% | 3.94 | 10.0% | 16.7% |
| Straight | 3 in a row, e.g. 2-3-4 | 15 pts | 15.3% | 8.83 | 41.1% | 5.30 | 26.9% | 11.1% |
| Sixes | Each 6 rolled | 4 pts per 6 | 7.3% | 4.18 | 11.3% | 3.87 | 8.0% | 19.4% |
| Ones | Each 1 rolled | 4 pts per 1 | 7.6% | 4.38 | 10.4% | 4.38 | 10.9% | 8.3% |
| Total | Any roll — never zero | Pays the total | 20.2% | 11.67 | 0.0% | 4.18 | 6.4% | 13.9% |

"First-roll home" is the share of all possible rolls the solver would place in that zone on an empty
board. Triple and Straight are the hold-open zones: filled late and often for nothing, as intended.

| Full-depth player vs itself | 0.0% / 100.0% / 0.0% | – |
| The House vs The House | 0.0% / 100.0% / 0.0% | – |
| Two near-experts (each slips 1 decision in 8) | 35.4% / 29.1% / 35.5% | – |

The full-depth player assumes its opponent follows the expected-score line and picks, at every roll, the
zone with the best chance of finishing ahead. Its mean score is lower than the solver's
(56.79 vs 57.64) and it still wins more often: it trades average points for the right
kind of variance. That is the interactive layer of the game, and it only exists because opponent
placements are revealed turn by turn.

Equal totals are broken by zones won. In solver-vs-greedy play 12.9% of matches finish level on points
and 12.1% remain drawn after the tie-break.

Both seats receive the same rolls and the scoring and tie-break code has no seat-dependent branch. The
deterministic pairing mirrors exactly when seats are swapped. Greedy breaks equal payouts at random from a
per-seat stream, so its rows differ by sampling noise only. Random-vs-random seat skew is
-0.06 percentage points, inside the 3σ noise band of ±0.94.

## What tuning changed

| Pay table | Small / Big | Double | Total | Solver vs random | Solver vs greedy (W / D / L) | Greedy gap | Largest zone share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brief (starting values) | 11 − total / total − 10 | 8 | half the total | 98.7% | 65.2% / 16.1% / 18.7% | +2.3 (5.2%) | 20.8% |
| Tuned (shipping) | 2 × that | 10 | the total | 98.8% | 70.1% / 12.1% / 17.8% | +3.7 (6.4%) | 20.2% |

Triple (30), Straight (15) and Sixes / Ones (4 per die) kept their starting values. The search covered
about 2,500 pay tables; doubling Small / Big and paying the full total on Total gives the greediest
player more tempting early cash-ins that the solver declines. Nothing in the searched space pushed the
solver much past a 75 / 15 split against greedy.

## Recommendations

- **Add one cross-zone rule if more depth is wanted.** A bonus that depends on two or more zones (for
  example, a payout for scoring in both Small and Big, or in every zone of a row) makes placements
  interact, which is what a greedy player cannot see. Point values alone will not get there.
- **Decide whether mid-match reveals stay.** Showing the opponent's board each turn is what gives the
  reading-the-opponent edge in check 4. Hiding boards until the end turns the game into two solitaire
  runs compared at the finish; simpler to explain, and identical optimal play then always draws.
- **Expect draws between strong players.** Two near-experts draw about a third of the time. If the
  platform needs a winner, add a second tie-break (for example fastest total decision time).
