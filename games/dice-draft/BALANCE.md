# Bad to the Bone — balance report

Generated by `node sim/balance.js --write`. 100,000 seeded matches for checks 1 and 2, fewer where stated, every one played
through the authoritative match layer (`js/match.js`) on the Arcade rules, spread over 50 dealt tables.
Win / draw / loss figures are from the first-named player's side.

## Verdict

| Check | Result | Headline |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Solver beats random | PASS | wins 100.0%, mean gap +153.6 pts |
| 2. Solver beats greedy | PASS | wins 79.5%, loses 19.3%, mean gap +20.1 pts |
| 3. No dominant zone or extra | PASS | the best zone earns 1.7× an average zone's points when dealt, the weakest 0.6×; the largest extra is 18% of all points |
| 4. Mirror match is not a tie | NOTE | identical solvers draw 100% (by construction); the bot that also uses Hold and Peek beats the plain solver 21.5% to 10.0% |
| 5. No seat advantage | PASS | swapping seats mirrors results exactly; random-vs-random seat A 49.7% vs seat B 49.8% |

Read these before relying on the table:

- **"Solver" is no longer provably optimal.** It is exact for everything except Hold and Peek: which roll
  to take, where to put it, and when to spend Flip and Double. The House adds Hold and Peek by searching
  one turn ahead, and beats it, so the true optimum is higher than either.
- **Check 4 does not hold as the original brief wrote it.** Two players with the same information and the
  same deterministic policy make identical moves and draw every time. That is true of any
  shared-sequence game.
- **The extras made the game deeper, and swingier.** Greedy play now scores 90% of the solver's points
  (it was 94% in the original game) and the one-step Grinder heuristic 93% (was 99%).
  Scores are about 3.4× larger and the multipliers stack, so single placements decide more.

## 5. Seat fairness

| Pairing | Seat A wins | Draw | Seat B wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solver (A) vs The House (B) | 9.4% | 68.8% | 21.8% |

| Random (A) vs random (B) | 49.7% | 0.5% | 49.8% |

Both seats receive the same table and the same rolls, and the scoring, bounty and tie-break code has no
seat-dependent branch. The deterministic pairing mirrors exactly when seats are swapped. Random-vs-random
seat skew is -0.15 percentage points, against a 3σ noise band of ±0.95.
